Night Float is a fictional, adjustable test economy. This guide explains what every colour, line, moving packet, balance, control, and financial word means—and what it does not prove.

How to use itBrowse all terms
THE ONE-SENTENCE VERSIONA short-duration partner facility puts e-float on selected cash-in-heavy tills before the morning queue, then asks for principal and a fee back at 18:00.

It is a simulation for discussion. It does not move real money.

A five-minute tour

How to use the control room

  1. 1
    Begin with Baseline.

    Watch tills refuse cash-ins and grey/red dashed emergency A2A transfers appear. This is the “without Night Float” comparison.

  2. 2
    Switch to Night Float.

    At 06:00, gold packets travel from the MMFL pool to eligible agents. Compare refused cash-ins against the same seeded Baseline.

  3. 3
    Move the time slider.

    Scrub from 05:45 through the morning peak, 16:00 sweep, and 18:00 settlement. Pausing never changes the precomputed result.

  4. 4
    Change one assumption.

    Try fee, demand, cash-out mix, default risk, book cap, or score threshold. Each change regenerates both comparison tapes.

  5. 5
    Break a dependency.

    Turn off MMFL, the funder, scoring, or the sweeper. The incident log explains the consequence, safeguard, residual exposure, and decision.

Visual language

What every colour and symbol means

Colour is never the only signal: the model also uses words, shapes, rings, line styles, incidents, and balance changes.

Green · healthy till

Enough combined own and idle facility e-float for expected near-term cash-ins. It does not mean the agent is profitable or risk-free.

Amber · e-float at risk

The till may run short soon. It is a warning before a refusal, not a default.

!

Red + ! · cash-in refused

A customer has notes but the agent lacks e-float. Red is also used for severe incidents and frozen/defaulted tills.

□

Purple + square · cash-out refused

The agent lacks physical notes. Night Float supplies e-float, so this failure may remain even when cash-in performance improves.

Gold · Night Float principal

An allocation packet or the active intervention line. Gold shows identified facility value moving; it does not mean money was created.

Blue · partner / legal context

Funder role, facility-active ring, assumptions, or unresolved customer classification. Blue is context—not proof of approval.

Solid blue ring · facility active

The till received approved facility principal.

Dull gold ring · idle allocation

Facility e-float was allocated but not consumed by cash-ins. Idle principal earns no facility fee.

Grey dashed ring · price declined

The agent was considered but rejected the fee, so no allocation was sent.

×

Red cross · frozen till

Principal was not fully returned at settlement. The till is excluded from tomorrow’s allocation file.

Small vertical bar · drawer cash

Physical notes at the till. It rises on cash-in and falls on cash-out or a sweep.

OK

BOOKS OK / BROKEN

Both accounting identities reconcile within GHS 0.02. BROKEN stops the model; it is never allowed to continue silently.

Direction matters

How to read every flow

MMFL POOL→AGENT TILL

06:00 · allocation travels outward

A solid gold route from pool to till means approved principal is being allocated. The funding vault stays static; the path does not originate at the vault.

AGENT A⇢AGENT B

Morning · emergency A2A

A dashed grey/red route between agents exists only in Baseline. It is capped at GHS 400, delayed 15 minutes, and limited to two received hops.

AGENT CASH→SWEEPER

16:00–18:00 · notes support settlement

A dashed green route means the sweeper converts only existing drawer notes above the GHS 500 reserve into e-value. No notes means no sweep.

AGENT TILL→MMFL POOL

18:00 · settlement returns inward

A green return route carries paid principal and fee toward the pool. A shortfall turns the till into a frozen exception.

CUSTOMER COHORT→LOCK RECORD

21:00 · hypothesis lock record

Only customer-supported hypothesis mode shows this aggregate record. The legal claim stays “still-customer”; the model does not establish that reuse is lawful.

FUNDER VAULT·STATIC

No movement · a deliberate legal picture

The funder role may support capacity, but operational allocation does not animate the vault or change the safeguarded customer balance.

Reading the evidence

The four headline numbers

01

Refused cash-ins

The primary outcome. “Avoided” equals Baseline cash-in refusals minus active-run cash-in refusals, using the same seed. Cash-out refusals are excluded.

02

Facility utilised

Used principal divided by allocated principal. A low number means capital sat idle, even if many gold packets appeared.

03

Settled principal

The share of allocated principal returned at 18:00. The supporting line shows frozen tills and explained principal loss.

04

System result

Fees on utilised principal minus unresolved default loss. Fee shares explain distribution; they are not extra value.

Plain-language dictionary

Every important keyword

/

A2A (agent-to-agent)
An emergency transfer of e-float from one agent to another. Baseline uses strict limits so A2A helps without magically solving every shortage.
Allocated principal
Facility money approved and placed on an agent position. It may later be used, remain idle, be repaid, or default.
Assumption
A number or rule chosen for the fictional model, not a fact supplied by MMFL, a bank, BoG, or real customers.
Avoided cash-in refusals
Baseline refused cash-ins minus active-run refused cash-ins. A positive result means the intervention served requests the identical Baseline did not.
Baseline
The same market and demand without scheduled Night Float allocation. Limited emergency A2A remains active so it is a fairer comparison.
Book cap
The maximum total facility principal the fictional economy may allocate. It prevents the model from solving shortages with unlimited money.
Books / accounting identity
A machine check that every cedi has an explained location or loss. Two separate identities cover facility principal and customer safeguarding.
Cash-in
A customer gives notes to an agent and receives wallet value. It consumes agent e-float and increases drawer cash.
Cash-out
A customer gives wallet value and receives notes. It consumes drawer cash and increases the agent’s own e-float.
Customer legal claim
The amount still owed to customers. In the hypothesis it remains tagged “still-customer,” even when a lock record exists.
Customer-supported hypothesis
An unresolved alternative where opted-in balances are modelled as support. It is not the default and does not establish lawfulness.
Default / principal shortfall
The part of allocated principal not returned at 18:00. The model explains it as loss, freezes the till, and removes it from tomorrow’s file.
Demand
The seeded number and size of cash-in and cash-out requests. The demand slider scales both sides while preserving comparison shocks.
Drawer cash
Physical banknotes held by an agent. They are not the same thing as wallet value or e-float.
E-float
Electronic value an agent uses to credit customer wallets during cash-in. A cash-in-heavy till can have plenty of notes but too little e-float.
Facility fee
The one adjustable price paid on principal actually utilised. Idle allocation is not charged.
fee_share
The model’s name for a customer portion of agent fees in the unresolved hypothesis. It is deliberately not called interest.
Funding partner
The regulated role that supplies or guarantees partner-backed capacity. Turning it off makes partner-mode capacity zero.
Incident
A timestamped explanation of a failure or constraint, including consequence, safeguard, remaining exposure, and decision needed.
Idle principal
Allocated facility e-float that has not been used by cash-ins. It appears as a dull gold ring and earns no fee.
Legal claim
Who is still entitled to the value. A legal claim is different from the operational place where an animation is drawn.
MMFL rails / pool
The modelled operational role that authorises and routes allocations. If rails are off, there is no capacity and no gold allocation packet.
Monte Carlo
Repeated random simulation. Night Float does not run a live Monte Carlo during the pitch; it uses a fixed seeded tape so results do not jump.
Opt-in
The share of customers in the lock-eligible subset. It affects only customer-supported hypothesis mode, never partner-backed capacity.
Packet
A visible aggregate unit moving between model entities. Every packet carries source, legal claim, state, amount, endpoints, and timing.
Partner-backed facility
The default model: regulated partner principal supports approved agent e-float, without treating customer balances as the facility.
Principal
The original facility amount that must be accounted for separately from fees.
Refusal
A customer request the agent cannot complete. Red means insufficient e-float for cash-in; purple means insufficient notes for cash-out.
Risk score
A fixed 0–1 eligibility lamp. It is not artificial intelligence or a learning model. If the service is off, a stricter rule is used.
Safeguarding
Keeping customer claims matchedable to protected value. The model checks this separately from facility principal so they cannot be double-counted.
Seed 2026
The fixed starting number for fictional randomness. The same controls and seed produce the same agents, demand, and result every time.
Settlement
The 18:00 return of facility principal and fee. Returned, defaulted, and frozen outcomes are recorded separately.
Stress scenario
A preset with more default risk, no sweeper, a smaller cap, a named risk signal, settlement loss, and next-day exclusion.
Super-agent / sweeper
The route that turns real drawer notes above the reserve into e-value for settlement between 16:00 and 18:00.
Utilised principal
Facility e-float actually consumed after an agent’s own e-float runs out. This—not total allocation—is the fee basis.
Vault
The safeguarded or funding balance picture. It intentionally stays still when operational e-float is allocated, preventing a misleading legal story.
Wind tunnel
A safe model for changing assumptions and seeing consequences. Like an aircraft wind tunnel, it tests ideas without pretending to be production.

Common questions

What should I conclude?

Is Night Float already connected to MTN MoMo?

No. The site is an offline fictional economy. Any real facility, wallet movement, data use, or partner branding requires the appropriate partner and regulatory process.

Does a better result prove the product will work?

No. It shows which assumptions would make the idea plausible and which data must be validated. The model is designed to be challenged.

Why can cash-out tills stay purple or red?

They need physical notes. Night Float supplies e-float for cash-in demand. A different cash-distribution intervention is needed for cash-out shortages.

Why is customer-supported mode labelled unresolved?

A customer claim cannot be treated as both immediately redeemable and freely reused without an approved legal, ledger, liquidity, and loss structure.

Where does the modelled profit come from?

Only from the facility fee on utilised principal, less unresolved default loss. Idle money earns no fee and recipient splits do not create extra money.
