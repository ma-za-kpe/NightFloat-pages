  3. 3

  4. 4

  5. 5

The shop problem · no finance language

One kiosk, two kinds of stock.

Think of a MoMo agent as a shop with two shelves. One shelf holds digital value. The other holds physical notes. A busy agent can have a full shelf on one side and an empty shelf on the other.

01

Digital shelf · e-float

Needed when a customer brings cash in.

Customer gives notes→Agent credits the phone

The agent’s e-float falls and the drawer fills with notes. If the digital shelf is empty, the cash-in is refused—even when the drawer is full.

02

Physical shelf · drawer cash

Needed when a customer wants cash out.

Customer sends wallet value→Agent hands over notes

The drawer falls and the agent’s own e-float rises. Night Float does not manufacture notes, so it cannot solve this shortage.

NIGHT FLOAT’S NARROW TARGET Cash-in-heavy market corridors at dawn: notes are arriving, but agents need digital stock before the first queue.

A payday estate that mainly needs notes is the wrong corridor. A product that claims to fix both shelves is hiding the problem.

A day in three people

The machine, seen from the street.

The same day feels different depending on which side of the kiosk you stand. The default story below uses regulated partner funding, not customer balances.

AMA · TOMATO SELLER

She is the outcome.

At 06:12 Ama brings yesterday’s notes to put on MoMo before paying her supplier. Success is simple: Kojo serves her instead of saying “no float.”

KOJO · CASH-IN AGENT

He needs stock before opening.

Kojo requests GHS 1,500 for 06:00 and sees the full fee before he accepts. Cash-ins use the e-float and leave notes in his drawer. By 18:00 he must return principal plus the disclosed fee.

ESI · MMFL PRODUCT OWNER

She owns the rules and the stop button.

Esi approves the cap, agent file, customer and agent wording, allowed rail, settlement rule, incident report, and pilot kill criteria. Night Float operates the clock she approves.

CUSTOMER-LOCK VERSION · HYPOTHESIS ONLY “Efua locks GHS 50 overnight” is an attractive story, but it is not the default legal product.

Whether a customer claim may support agent liquidity, how redemption remains guaranteed, and whether any customer fee_share is permitted must be designed and approved by MMFL, its bank, and the regulator. Until then, the hero tape is partner-backed.

The eleven holes · kitchen language

Where the pipe can leak.

These are not footnotes. Each hole is either visible in the model, a proposed safeguard to validate, or a question that can kill the pilot.

01

It looks like a bank.

Keep MMFL as licensed principal, a regulated partner on capacity, and Night Float on timing and matching only.

02

Dawn works; dusk fails.

Cash-in leaves the agent with notes, not repayable e-value. No approved reverse rail means there is only half a product.

03

The wrong wallet pipe is used.

Never assume ordinary customer→agent or A2A limits disappear. MMFL must approve the exact tagged pool↔agent route.

04

Someone wants money at 05:50.

Partner-backed mode avoids customer lock risk. Any future customer lock needs an explicit availability promise and liquidity protection.

05

An agent disappears.

Cap exposure, bind device and identity, watch post-load activity, freeze early, and write loss ownership into the contract.

06

Tax eats both trips.

Do not guess an e-levy or tax exemption. MMFL legal and tax must confirm the treatment of pool↔agent movements in writing.

07

The fee is wrong.

Too high and agents decline; too low and operations lose money. Show empty allocation slots and charge only utilised principal.

08

Existing partners feel attacked.

Super-agents, banks, and payment operators can own the evening route. The design should create a seat, not steal one.

09

People farm the incentive.

Proposed controls include one identity, device binding, small starting caps, and evidence of genuine wallet activity.

10

There is no data permission.

Ask for the minimum pilot view: agent identifier, cash-in versus cash-out by hour, and a coarse location grid.

11

The headline becomes the product.

Say “capped agent facility” and “still your claim.” Never lead with customer yield or pretend an unresolved lock is approved.

Visual language

What every colour and symbol means

Colour is never the only signal: the model also uses words, shapes, rings, line styles, incidents, and balance changes.

Green · healthy till

Enough combined own and idle facility e-float for expected near-term cash-ins. It does not mean the agent is profitable or risk-free.

Amber · e-float at risk

The till may run short soon. It is a warning before a refusal, not a default.

!

Red + ! · cash-in refused

A customer has notes but the agent lacks e-float. Red is also used for severe incidents and frozen/defaulted tills.

□

Purple + square · cash-out refused

The agent lacks physical notes. Night Float supplies e-float, so this failure may remain even when cash-in performance improves.

Gold · Night Float principal

An allocation packet or the active intervention line. Gold shows identified facility value moving; it does not mean money was created.

Blue · partner / legal context

Funder role, facility-active ring, assumptions, or unresolved customer classification. Blue is context—not proof of approval.

Solid blue ring · facility active

The till received approved facility principal.

Dull gold ring · idle allocation

Facility e-float was allocated but not consumed by cash-ins. Idle principal earns no facility fee.

Grey dashed ring · price declined

The agent was considered but rejected the fee, so no allocation was sent.

×

Red cross · frozen till

Principal was not fully returned at settlement. The till is excluded from tomorrow’s allocation file.

Small vertical bar · drawer cash

Physical notes at the till. It rises on cash-in and falls on cash-out or a sweep.

OK

RECONCILED / UNRESOLVED / BROKEN

Principal, fee, and safeguarding arithmetic reconcile within GHS 0.02. Customer-supported funding remains UNRESOLVED until a separate asset and loss bearer exist. BROKEN stops the model.

Direction matters

How to read every flow

MMFL POOL→AGENT TILL

06:00 · allocation travels outward

A solid gold route from pool to till means approved principal is being allocated. The funding vault stays static; the path does not originate at the vault.

AGENT A⇢AGENT B

Morning · emergency A2A

A dashed grey/red route between agents exists only in Baseline. It is capped at GHS 400, delayed 15 minutes, and limited to two received hops.

AGENT CASH→SWEEPER

16:00–18:00 · notes support settlement

A dashed green route means the sweeper converts only existing drawer notes above the GHS 500 reserve into e-value. No notes means no sweep.

AGENT TILL→MMFL POOL

18:00 · settlement returns inward

A green return route carries paid principal and fee toward the pool. A shortfall turns the till into a frozen exception.

CUSTOMER COHORT→LOCK RECORD

21:00 · hypothesis lock record

Only customer-supported hypothesis mode shows this aggregate record. The legal claim stays “still-customer”; the model does not establish that reuse is lawful.

FUNDER VAULT·STATIC

No movement · a deliberate legal picture

The funder role may support capacity, but operational allocation does not animate the vault or change the safeguarded customer balance.

Reading the evidence

The four headline numbers

01

Refused cash-ins

The primary outcome. “Avoided” equals Baseline cash-in refusals minus active-run cash-in refusals, using the same seed. Cash-out refusals are excluded.

02

Facility utilised

Used principal divided by allocated principal. A low number means capital sat idle, even if many gold packets appeared.

03

Settled principal

The share of allocated principal returned at 18:00. The supporting line shows frozen tills and explained principal loss.

04

Cash result before costs

Fees actually collected by 18:00 minus unresolved principal. It excludes funding, sweep, tax, fraud, recovery, and platform costs, so it is not profit.

Plain-language dictionary

Every important keyword

/

A2A (agent-to-agent)
An emergency transfer of e-float from one agent to another. Baseline uses strict limits so A2A helps without magically solving every shortage.
Allocated principal
Facility money approved and placed on an agent position. It may later be used, remain idle, be repaid, or default.
Assumption
A number or rule chosen for the fictional model, not a fact supplied by MMFL, a bank, BoG, or real customers.
Avoided cash-in refusals
Baseline refused cash-ins minus active-run refused cash-ins. A positive result means the intervention served requests the identical Baseline did not.
Baseline
The same market and demand without scheduled Night Float allocation. Limited emergency A2A remains active so it is a fairer comparison.
Book cap
The maximum total facility principal the fictional economy may allocate. It prevents the model from solving shortages with unlimited money.
Books / accounting identity
A machine check that every cedi has an explained location or loss. Two separate identities cover facility principal and customer safeguarding.
Cash-in
A customer gives notes to an agent and receives wallet value. It consumes agent e-float and increases drawer cash.
Cash-out
A customer gives wallet value and receives notes. It consumes drawer cash and increases the agent’s own e-float.
Customer legal claim
The amount still owed to customers. In the hypothesis it remains tagged “still-customer,” even when a lock record exists.
Customer-supported hypothesis
An unresolved alternative where opted-in balances are modelled as support. It is not the default and does not establish lawfulness.
Default / principal shortfall
The part of allocated principal not returned at 18:00. The model explains it as loss, freezes the till, and removes it from tomorrow’s file.
Demand
The seeded number and size of cash-in and cash-out requests. The demand slider scales both sides while preserving comparison shocks.
Drawer cash
Physical banknotes held by an agent. They are not the same thing as wallet value or e-float.
E-float
Electronic value an agent uses to credit customer wallets during cash-in. A cash-in-heavy till can have plenty of notes but too little e-float.
Facility fee
The one adjustable price paid on principal actually utilised. Idle allocation is not charged.
fee_share
The model’s name for a customer portion of agent fees in the unresolved hypothesis. It is deliberately not called interest.
Funding partner
The regulated role that supplies or guarantees partner-backed capacity. Turning it off makes partner-mode capacity zero.
Incident
A timestamped explanation of a failure or constraint, including consequence, safeguard, remaining exposure, and decision needed.
Idle principal
Allocated facility e-float that has not been used by cash-ins. It appears as a dull gold ring and earns no fee.
Legal claim
Who is still entitled to the value. A legal claim is different from the operational place where an animation is drawn.
MMFL rails / pool
The modelled operational role that authorises and routes allocations. If rails are off, there is no capacity and no gold allocation packet.
Monte Carlo
Repeated random simulation. Night Float does not run a live Monte Carlo during the pitch; it uses a fixed seeded tape so results do not jump.
Opt-in
The share of customers in the lock-eligible subset. It affects only customer-supported hypothesis mode, never partner-backed capacity.
Packet
A visible aggregate unit moving between model entities. Every packet carries source, legal claim, state, amount, endpoints, and timing.
Partner-backed facility
The default model: regulated partner principal supports approved agent e-float, without treating customer balances as the facility.
Principal
The original facility amount that must be accounted for separately from fees.
Refusal
A customer request the agent cannot complete. Red means insufficient e-float for cash-in; purple means insufficient notes for cash-out.
Risk score
A fixed 0–1 eligibility lamp. It is not artificial intelligence or a learning model. If the service is off, a stricter rule is used.
Safeguarding
Keeping customer claims matchedable to protected value. The model checks this separately from facility principal so they cannot be double-counted.
Seed 2026
The fixed starting number for fictional randomness. The same controls and seed produce the same agents, demand, and result every time.
Settlement
The 18:00 return of facility principal and fee. Returned, defaulted, and frozen outcomes are recorded separately.
Stress scenario
A preset with more default risk, no sweeper, a smaller cap, a named risk signal, settlement loss, and next-day exclusion.
Super-agent / sweeper
The route that turns real drawer notes above the reserve into e-value for settlement between 16:00 and 18:00.
Utilised principal
Facility e-float actually consumed after an agent’s own e-float runs out. This—not total allocation—is the fee basis.
Vault
The safeguarded or funding balance picture. It intentionally stays still when operational e-float is allocated, preventing a misleading legal story.
Wind tunnel
A safe model for changing assumptions and seeing consequences. Like an aircraft wind tunnel, it tests ideas without pretending to be production.
Agent bid
An agent’s request for a stated amount of dawn e-float at a stated fee. In a real pilot the agent must see and accept the price; the model never forces acceptance.
Agent liability
The contractual responsibility created when an agent uses and fails to return facility principal. The model can expose a shortfall, but MMFL and the funding parties must write who ultimately absorbs it.
BoG / FIO
Bank of Ghana and the relevant fintech or innovation function in the proposed pilot conversation. They set or interpret the regulatory path; the simulation does not speak for them.
Cash-in belt
A corridor where many customers bring notes to put on their phones, especially near opening time. It is the target geography; a mainly cash-out area is not.
Device binding
A proposed safeguard that connects an approved identity or agent position to a known device. It can make duplicate or suspicious participation harder; it does not eliminate fraud.
Dusk reverse rail
The approved route that turns real drawer notes into e-value before settlement. Without this evening route, a dawn facility is only half a product.
Geofence
A coarse approved location boundary used to keep a pilot inside one selected market corridor. It is a scope control, not a claim of precise surveillance.
Ghana Card cap
A proposed identity-linked participation limit. The regulator and MMFL would decide the real identity, privacy, and cap rules; the model only shows why duplication needs control.
Hubtel
An illustrative payment-operations or cash-point candidate for the evening rail. It is not presented as a confirmed partner or as proof that a suitable service already exists.
Liquidity tag
A proposed machine-readable label that distinguishes an approved pool↔agent liquidity movement from an ordinary person-to-person transfer. MMFL must confirm the real mechanism.
Master-agent-class pool
Shorthand for the specially controlled operational role the research wants MMFL to validate. The phrase is not proof that the proposed use is already licensed or exempt from limits.
No-go gate
A condition that stops the real pilot before money moves. No sandbox path, no approved data, or no approved pool rail means the concept remains a simulation.
Statutory trust interest
Interest generated under the existing safeguarded-float arrangement, if and as Ghanaian rules require. It is separate from Night Float’s modelled facility fee and must not be quietly combined with fee_share.
Trust account
The protected bank-account structure associated with customer e-money safeguarding. A static vault picture reminds the audience that an operational e-float allocation is not permission to spend customer money.
Fees due / accrued fees
Utilised principal multiplied by the disclosed daily fee. It is what agents owe, not proof cash arrived. Collected cash and unpaid receivables are tracked separately.
Default loss
Facility principal that has not been returned and is treated as unresolved principal in the model. It is subtracted from collected fees in the cash result before costs.
Night Float platform share
The modelled 22% portion of collected fees assigned to Night Float. It is cash allocation before expenses—not profit.
Revenue
Money assigned to a participant before that participant’s own costs. Night Float’s fee share is modelled revenue; it does not show what the company keeps.
Profit
Revenue left after every real expense and loss. The v1 simulation does not calculate Night Float company profit because it has no salaries, legal bills, tax, insurance, hosting, or support budget.
Cash result before costs
Collected fees minus unresolved principal for the selected horizon. It omits operating and funding costs, is not Night Float’s earnings, and is not audited accounting profit.
Projection horizon
The reporting window selected in the dropdown: 1, 7, 30, 90, 180, or 365 modeled days. It repeats today’s assumptions; it does not claim to know what will actually happen next year.
Modeled day
One complete 21:00-to-20:45 run of the fictional economy. Longer horizons repeat its flow totals while leaving percentage rates unchanged.
Agent-day
One agent participating for one modeled day. Four accepted agents repeated over seven days equals 28 accepted agent-days—not 28 different people.
Freeze event
One modeled failure that freezes a till for the next allocation file. A projected count is repeated-day exposure, not a count of unique agents and not a claim that tomorrow was simulated.

Seventeen honest answers

What people will ask next.

These answers separate what the simulation demonstrates, what the proposed pilot would need, and what only a regulator or licensed partner can decide.

Is Night Float already connected to MTN MoMo?

No. The site is an offline fictional economy. Any real facility, wallet movement, data use, or partner branding requires the appropriate partner and regulatory process.

Does a better result prove the product will work?

No. It shows which assumptions would make the idea plausible and which data must be validated. The model is designed to be challenged.

Who owns the customer’s money?

The customer keeps the claim against the licensed issuer under the applicable safeguarding arrangement. Night Float never claims to own, hold, or issue that value. In the default partner-backed model, customer balances are not the facility principal.

Is this a bank, savings account, or public lending club?

No. The proposed default is a short agent facility supplied through regulated partners. Night Float would provide matching, scheduling, geofencing, monitoring, and an explainable ledger—not deposits, e-money, or a public balance sheet.

Does Night Float solve cash-out shortages?

They need physical notes. Night Float supplies e-float for cash-in demand. A different cash-distribution intervention is needed for cash-out shortages.

Why is the evening sweep as important as dawn allocation?

Cash-in consumes e-float and leaves notes in the agent’s drawer. Notes cannot settle a digital facility by themselves. An approved bank, payment operator, or super-agent route must turn real notes back into e-value before 18:00. Dawn without dusk is half a product.

What happens if an agent does not repay?

The model records the shortfall, freezes the till, and removes it from tomorrow’s allocation file. A real contract must define recovery, agent liability, any commission offset, and who ultimately absorbs loss. The startup must not invent that answer.

Why is customer-supported mode labelled unresolved?

A customer claim cannot be treated as both immediately redeemable and freely reused without an approved legal, ledger, liquidity, and loss structure.

Why not promise users “interest while you sleep”?

That framing can make the concept sound like deposit-taking or a yield product, while the amount may be too small to motivate anyone. The model therefore calls any customer portion fee_share, keeps it off the default tape, and leaves permission to MMFL, its bank, and BoG.

Where does the cash result before costs come from?

Only from fees collected by 18:00, less unresolved principal. Idle money earns no fee, unpaid fee stays a receivable, and recipient splits do not create extra cash.

Is the displayed collected-fee amount Night Float’s daily profit?

No. Fees due, fees collected, and unpaid fees are different. Only collected fees are split; Night Float receives 22% of that cash before paying its own costs. This model does not calculate profit.

Does the one-year view predict an actual year?

No. It repeats the selected one-day tape 365 times so you can see the arithmetic consequence of the current fee, demand, default, corridor, cap, and partner settings. It does not invent growth, weekends, new customers, changing rates, or tomorrow’s agent list. Treat it as a transparent sensitivity projection, not a forecast.

Are BoG, MMFL, Jumo, Hubtel, or a bank already partners?

No endorsement or signed relationship is claimed. The page names desired roles and illustrative candidates so the partnership ask is concrete. Every real participant, service, licence interpretation, and logo use requires validation.

What about e-levy or other transfer taxes?

The simulation does not calculate tax or claim an exemption. MMFL legal and tax advisers must confirm the treatment of the exact pool↔agent movements. If round-trip tax destroys the economics, that is a kill condition—not a number to hide.

What pilot data is actually needed?

Start narrow: an approved agent identifier, hourly cash-in versus cash-out totals, and a coarse location grid for one market. That is enough to test whether a dawn cash-in belt exists without asking for an entire customer data lake.

Will banks and super-agents lose their business?

The design should give them the dusk job: facility capacity, physical-cash collection, e-value rebuilding, settlement support, or a standby line. Night Float matches and schedules; it should not pretend existing balance-sheet and cash-network work disappears.

What must MMFL confirm before a real pilot?

The sandbox path, exact pool/liquidity rail, customer and agent wording, pilot data access, named product and network owners, tax treatment, loss ownership, and the approved dusk rail. Without the sandbox, data, and pool rail, the real pilot is a no-go.
